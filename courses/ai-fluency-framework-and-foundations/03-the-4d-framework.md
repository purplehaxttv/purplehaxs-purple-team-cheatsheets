## The point

Whichever mode you're using to work with AI — Automation, Augmentation, or Agency — four competencies determine whether that collaboration goes well: **Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence**. This lesson defines each one precisely, in depth, for the first time.

## The four competencies, one at a time

**Delegation** — the big picture: what are you trying to accomplish, what work is involved, what should *you* handle vs. AI? Example: on a research project, you might have AI review lengthy documents/data and discuss implications, but reserve critical analysis and final conclusions for yourself. Requires understanding your goal, recognizing what AI can/can't do well, and thoughtfully dividing the work. **Delegation isn't just offloading tasks — it's having a clear vision and strategically choosing how AI fits into your process.**

**Description** — clear communication with AI. Contrast: vaguely saying "make me a logo" vs. describing company values, target audience, colors, style references. Or as a tutor: "don't tell me the answer, help me work through this step by step." Goes beyond prompt-writing — detailed, context-rich conversations establishing the goal, output format, how the AI should approach the task, needed context, and tone/style of interaction.

**Discernment** — thoughtfully evaluating what AI gives you. Example: an AI-suggested marketing strategy — are the facts accurate, does the reasoning hold up, do recommendations align with brand values/audience, does this actually move you forward? Draws on your own domain expertise; the judgment to separate useful from not, and recognize when output needs refinement or should be discarded entirely. **Most AI interactions are small loops of Description and Discernment** — describe, evaluate, refine, repeat.

**Diligence** — responsible AI interaction. Example: writing job descriptions or reviewing applications with AI — how are you ensuring fairness and controlling for bias? Verifying accuracy before decisions? Protecting sensitive data? Being transparent about AI's involvement? Willing to be accountable for the AI-assisted work? **Diligence means taking ownership and being willing to stand behind the final product.**

## Why these four, specifically

The competencies apply across all three engagement modes (Automation, Augmentation, Agency) — none of the four is tied to a specific AI tool or technique that could go stale. That's the explicit design claim: they're "fundamental skills that will help you adapt and grow alongside this rapidly evolving technology," not a snapshot of today's best practices.

## Ties to the 4Ds

This *is* the 4Ds lesson — the first full definition of all four, each reduced to one governing question: Delegation (what work, whose?), Description (how do I communicate it?), Discernment (is this any good?), Diligence (am I being responsible?).

## Key takeaway to land

None of the 4Ds is optional and none stands alone — Delegation sets the scope, Description sets up the interaction, Discernment checks the output, Diligence takes responsibility for it. They're deliberately tool-agnostic: the claim is that they'll still matter after today's specific AI products are gone.

## Exercise: Apply the 4Ds

**Why:** to practice mapping all four competencies onto a single realistic scenario before the course goes deep on each one individually.

Pick one of three scenarios and work through all 4Ds against it:

1. **Communication project** — drafting marketing campaign emails with AI.
   - Delegation: what would you handle yourself vs. collaborate on?
   - Description: how would you communicate tone, purpose, success criteria?
   - Discernment: what criteria would judge whether the drafts meet your needs?
   - Diligence: what transparency/responsibility considerations matter here?
2. **Research project** — using AI to analyze a large dataset for a paper.
   - Delegation: how would you divide the analytical work?
   - Description: what context would the AI need about your research question?
   - Discernment: how would you verify the AI's analysis for accuracy?
   - Diligence: what ethical considerations arise when publishing AI-assisted research?
3. **Creative project** — developing story character concepts with AI.
   - Delegation: what creative elements go through AI vs. develop independently?
   - Description: how would you guide the AI toward characters fitting your story's world?
   - Discernment: how would you decide what to keep, modify, or discard?
   - Diligence: how would you acknowledge AI's contribution to the creative work?
