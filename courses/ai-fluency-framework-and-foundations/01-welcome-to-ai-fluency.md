## The point

This course isn't about AI as a technology — it's about *us*: how humans interact and collaborate with AI systems. The explicit ambition is to treat AI "not as some sort of next-level spellchecker, but instead as a trusted partner for doing creative and innovative problem-solving work," approached "confidently and capably."

## Why a framework, not a tips list

- The AI world moves fast enough that it's hard to know where to start or keep up *without* a grounding framework — otherwise you're chasing "the next prompt engineering fad."
- **AI Fluency** = the ability to engage with AI systems in ways that are **effective, efficient, ethical, and safe**.
- Not just technical definitions or prompting tips that could be outdated next month — "fundamentally shifting how we approach AI... so that we can learn how to think *with* AI."
- The stated problem this solves: organizations adopting AI without clear strategy, people frustrated with systems they don't fully understand, and a widening gap between what's *possible* and what feels comfortable/intuitive.

## Course arc (as stated in the lesson)

1. Overview of the AI Fluency framework — a model for human-AI interaction.
2. The ways we interact with AI systems, and the **four core competencies (the 4Ds)** that turn those interactions into something richer.
3. Each competency examined closely:
   - **Delegation** — when should humans do the work, and when should AI?
   - **Description** — how do we communicate clearly with AI systems?
   - **Discernment** — how do we evaluate what AI gives us?
   - **Diligence** — how do we ensure our interaction with AI is responsible, transparent, and accountable?
4. Real capabilities/limitations, and practical techniques applying the 4Ds.

Deliberate contrast drawn in the lesson itself: most AI training focuses on *tactical* skills (specific prompts, settings, tools) that go stale quickly. This course focuses on *core competencies and deep understanding* — built to hold up not just for today's AI, but for what comes next.

## Ties to the 4Ds

This lesson doesn't apply the 4Ds yet — it names and defines them as the scaffolding for the entire course: **Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence**, each framed as a single guiding question (above). Everything from here on builds out one or more of these four.

## Key takeaway to land

AI Fluency isn't a technology briefing — it's a claim that collaborating well with AI is a *skill*, built from four competencies (Delegation, Description, Discernment, Diligence), each reducible to one guiding question, that should hold up regardless of which model or tool you're using next year.
